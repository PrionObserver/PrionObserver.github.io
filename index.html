Fusion HV R&D Technician Study Plan

1. Critical Areas to Understand

Based on the job description and your resume, prioritize brushing up on the following. Pay attention to the mentioned standards where applicable.

  • High Voltage (HV) Fundamentals:

    • Concepts: Basic principles of electric fields, dielectric strength, breakdown voltage, corona discharge, creepage, and clearance distances.
    • Safety: Deep understanding of HV safety protocols, lockout/tagout procedures in HV environments, proper grounding techniques, use of insulating tools and personal protective equipment (PPE). This is paramount. Familiarize yourself with key concepts from NFPA 70E.
    • Insulation: Types of HV insulation materials (solids, liquids, gases), their properties, and common failure mechanisms. Understanding how insulation is designed and maintained in HV systems.

  • Pulsed Power Systems & Components:

    • Concepts: What is pulsed power? Why is it used in fusion research? Basic understanding of pulse forming networks.
    • Key Components:
      • Capacitors: Types (e.g., pulsed discharge capacitors), their characteristics, charging/discharging cycles, and failure modes in pulsed applications.
      • Switches: Operating principles of high-speed, high-current switches like SCRs (Silicon Controlled Rectifiers) and IGBTs (Insulated Gate Bipolar Transistors). Their role in rapidly discharging energy.
      • Marx Generators: Understanding the basic circuit topology and principle of operation for generating high voltage pulses by charging capacitors in parallel and discharging them in series.
      • Pulse Transformers: Their function in stepping up voltage or current in pulsed power systems, insulation challenges.
    • Operation: How these components work together to generate specific voltage and current pulses.

  • HV Diagnostic Tools & Waveform Analysis:

    • Oscilloscopes: Advanced usage for capturing and analyzing high-speed, high-voltage, and high-current waveforms. Understanding triggering, sampling rates, bandwidth, and probe types (especially HV probes and current probes).
    • Waveform Analysis: Interpreting key characteristics of pulses (rise time, fall time, pulse width, amplitude, ringing, overshoot). Relating waveform anomalies to potential component issues.

  • Troubleshooting HV/Pulsed Power Systems:

    • Combining your existing troubleshooting methodology with specific considerations for HV (safety first, discharge procedures, insulation checks, component-specific testing).
    • Systematic approach to isolating faults in complex electrical and pulsed power circuits.

  • Technical Documentation:

    • Blueprint/Schematic Reading: Interpreting detailed electrical schematics, wiring diagrams, and mechanical drawings specific to complex R&D hardware. Understanding standard symbols for electrical components (capacitors, switches, transformers, resistors, etc.) and mechanical features. Review standard electrical symbols (e.g., per ANSI Y32.2 or IEEE 315).
    • Manuals: Efficiently extracting information from equipment manuals for operation, maintenance, and troubleshooting.

  • Relevant Standards (General Awareness):

    (While you likely won't need to memorize specific clauses, being aware of relevant standards shows professionalism.)

    • Electrical Safety Standards: NFPA 70E: Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace. This is a critical standard for working with electrical systems, especially HV. Focus on the principles of hazard identification, risk assessment, and selecting appropriate PPE.
    • Quality Management: ISO 9001: Quality Management Systems. While not a technical safety standard, awareness of its principles (focus on procedures, documentation, continuous improvement, customer satisfaction - in this case, internal "customers" like engineers) is beneficial in an R&D/production environment.
    • Machining Standards: Basic awareness of ANSI standards related to dimensions, tolerances, and fits (e.g., ANSI B4.1) is useful if discussing fabrication work.
    • General Safety Standards: OSHA regulations related to general workplace safety and specific electrical hazards.

2. Daily Study Checklist (Pre-Interview Focus)

Use this as a guide for focused study in the days or hours leading up to your interview.

  • Day 1-2 (Focus: Fundamentals & Safety)

    • Review core HV safety principles and procedures. Read introductory material on NFPA 70E.
    • Study basic concepts of electric fields, dielectric strength, insulation types.
    • Mentally walk through a safe HV system power-down and lockout/tagout process, referencing NFPA 70E principles.
    • Review your resume, specifically highlighting HV projects and safety mentions.
    • Practice answering the "Prioritize Safety" question with a specific (even if hypothetical but realistic) example, mentioning your adherence to safety standards.

  • Day 3-4 (Focus: Pulsed Power Components)

    • Study the operating principles of capacitors (especially pulsed), SCRs, and IGBTs.
    • Learn the basic concept and circuit of a Marx Generator.
    • Understand the function of pulse transformers.
    • Relate these components to your existing knowledge of electrical circuits. Look up example datasheets for power SCRs and IGBTs.

  • Day 5-6 (Focus: Diagnostics & Troubleshooting)

    • Review advanced oscilloscope features relevant to capturing pulses (triggering modes, single-shot capture). Watch specific tutorials on using oscilloscopes for pulsed signals.
    • Study how to interpret pulsed waveforms.
    • Practice walking through your troubleshooting process for an electrical issue, specifically adding HV safety steps (per NFPA 70E principles) and component-level thinking.
    • Consider how you would approach troubleshooting a pulsed power system (e.g., checking capacitor charging, switch timing, pulse shape).

  • Day 7+ (Focus: Integration & Practice)

    • Review technical drawings/schematics – find examples online if needed to practice interpreting standard electrical and mechanical symbols. Look up guides on ANSI Y32.2 or IEEE 315 symbols.
    • Connect your machining/fabrication skills to how you might build or modify parts for HV/pulsed power systems, mentioning awareness of tolerances and standards like ANSI B4.1.
    • Practice answering *all* the tailored interview questions (both general and specific). Record yourself if possible to review your delivery.
    • Prepare thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer about the specific HV/pulsed power systems at Helion, their safety culture, the team structure, and typical R&D workflows.

Study Strategy:

  • Start with Safety: This is non-negotiable for an HV role. Ensure you are confident discussing safety protocols, referencing NFPA 70E principles.
  • Focus on the "Required Skills": Make sure you can speak to *all* items listed in the "Required Skills" section for the specific role you are interviewing for, linking them to your experience where possible.
  • Prioritize HV and Pulsed Power Components: These are central to the Fusion Technician (High Voltage R&D) role. Use datasheets to understand real-world component parameters.
  • Practice Explaining Concepts Simply: Be able to explain complex ideas (like how a Marx generator works or interpreting a pulsed waveform) in clear, understandable terms.
  • Use Your Projects as Examples: Continuously connect the job requirements back to your SEM, sputter coater, microwave electronics, and machining projects. These are your strongest selling points for demonstrating hands-on HV and R&D capability. Frame your experience using relevant technical terms and referencing safety practices.
